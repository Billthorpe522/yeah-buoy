§ 01 · The Problem

Boats that get used
are boats that move.

Most weekends, the kayak sits on the roof. Not because people don't want to paddle — because they can't, or shouldn't, or just don't have the energy after a long week.

"Go to the lake paddle when you want to.
Don't paddle when you don't want to."

That's it. The motor doesn't replace the paddle. It removes the friction between you and getting on the water — so the kayak becomes a Tuesday evening thing, not a Saturday-only thing.

Who it's for

  • Fishermen who want to cover ground without burning out
  • Older paddlers who love the water but hate the strain
  • Rehab / shoulder recovery folks who still want to fish
  • Weekend recreationals with inflatable kayaks
  • DIY makers who want to print a thruster, not buy one

§ 02 · Specifications

Built to be repaired
in the field.

Every part is either 3D-printed by hand, off-the-shelf from a hobby shop, or both. Nothing is soldered. Nothing is potted. If something breaks, you replace it with a screwdriver and the spare in your dry bag.

Battery
12.8V
LiFePO4 deep-cycle with 30A BMS. Off-the-shelf, field-swappable.
Capacity
22Ah
Multi-hour runtime at cruise speed. Add a parallel plug for more.
Propeller
6-blade
Ducted, conformal-coated, 3D-printed. High torque, low RPM.
Mount
tool-free
Velcro straps to any kayak, inflatable, or jon-boat transom.

§ 03 · How It Works

No switches.
No soldering.
Just plug and paddle.

1

Strap on

Velcro-mount the ducted thruster unit to your kayak's transom. No tools. No drilling. Slide off when you're done.

2

Plug in

Ring terminal onto the battery. XT60 into the motor. The low-voltage connectors are waterproof — drop them in the lake if you want.

3

Pair remote

Wireless throttle remote pairs to the case in seconds. Hold it like a TV remote. Throttle up.

4

Go

Cruise at 2 mph, multi-hour runtime. Kill switch on the case if you need to bail. Unplug, dry off, done.
